# The odds, honestly

Written 2026-09-13, six apps in. Re-read this at app 30.

"Success" means four different things here, and the odds are different for each.

## 100 apps shipped
Good odds. Six apps in seven months, but four of them in the last ten days once momentum hit.
Target is 100 by March 31, 2027: about 3.5 apps a week, roughly 25 hours per app at 80-90 hour weeks.
Aggressive but real. The tracker measures against that line every day.

The work ethic is not the risk. Ten years of factory weeks proves that. The risks are building the
wrong things and letting video editing eat build time.

## The channel makes money
Low odds. Most channels never hit the Partner threshold (1,000 subs, 4,000 watch hours). The ones
that do take one to two years and 100-plus videos, and a 10k-sub channel often earns a few hundred a
month in ads. The channel is marketing, not the business.

## The apps make money
Low per app, but it compounds. Most indie apps earn nothing. One in twenty or thirty earns real money.
That is the actual logic of building 100: a hundred tickets where the odds beat a lottery, and every
one teaches which tickets pay. The Kalshi bot losing forty dollars was a cheap lesson.

## Getting hired
Best odds, and it is not close. Shipped apps, a public repo, a tracker that shows scoping, costing,
and honest reporting, and a story that is easy to tell in an interview. Six months of this plus
LinkedIn updates should produce interviews for controls, automation, or junior dev roles even if the
channel and the apps make nothing.

## What tips the odds
- Build for people you already know. Plant floor apps have almost no competition and you understand the customer.
- Ship smaller. A weekend tool counts the same on the scoreboard as a month-long game.
- Charge money early. A five-dollar unlock on app 10 teaches more than free downloads on app 50.
- Keep the LinkedIn posts going every week or two. That audience hires. The YouTube audience watches.

## What kills it
Chasing view counts instead of shipped apps, and quitting between app 15 and 25 when the novelty is
gone and nothing has paid yet. That is where most people stop. Still shipping at app 30 means you are
past the point where most quit, and the odds shift.

## Channel structure
Two kinds of channel with two different jobs:
- Blue Collar to Code: the journey. One episode per app with the story and the numbers. Subscribers live here.
- One channel per app: the landing page. A demo, a how-to, the install link. Titled for search, no story.
  Search traffic does not need subscribers. Flock Camera Tracker already works this way.
Cross-link both directions. Create app channels as apps ship, not up front. Budget an hour each for setup.

## Kill rules, decided in advance (2026-09-13)

**Main channel, judged at video 100.** It must be in the YouTube Partner Program:
1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days).
Partner by video 100, or stop. No other outcome counts.

**Halfway check at video 50.** If nothing on the list is moving, change the format. Do not quit at 50.

**Per-app channels.** Three to five videos, then leave them alone. Judge at 90 days: installs coming
from search means add a video when the app updates. Otherwise it sits there for free. Never spend a
day on a video for an app nobody found.

**Build mode.** Pick by 10 AM, build by 6 PM, video up by 9 PM, never revisit a miss.
Lane: tools that let people check, track, count, or calculate something in the news. No sides taken.
Politics, health claims, and anything aimed at a group gets apps pulled and videos demonetized.
